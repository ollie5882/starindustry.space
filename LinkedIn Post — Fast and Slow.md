# LinkedIn Post — Fast and Slow

Ten days ago I had more or less decided to wind the company down.

Nothing dramatic. Just the quiet arithmetic every founder eventually does at 2am: what's actually here, what's actually coming, and how long the runway lasts if you're honest about both. The answer I kept arriving at was — keep one product, keep a bit of consultancy, let the rest go.

Today the picture is almost unrecognisable. There's a pipeline forming across both sides of the business: the physical parts we make, and the systems thinking we sell. Anchor customers, partners keen to build processes and parts with us, a new entrant asking for structural and thermal support, real potential on the architecture side. Not signed, not certain — but alive in a way it plainly wasn't ten days ago.

Here's the uncomfortable part: almost nothing fundamental changed in those ten days.

No breakthrough. No rescue deal. What changed was information and framing — a handful of conversations that reframed what was already there, and some slow-moving things that finally surfaced.

Daniel Kahneman split the mind into two systems. The fast one reacts — it feels the runway shrinking and starts writing the eulogy. The slow one reasons — it waits for evidence and separates a bad week from a bad business.

The trouble with running a company is that you feel every swing at the speed of the fast brain, and you have to make decisions at the speed of the slow one. Ten days ago my fast brain was completely in charge. It was certain. It was also wrong.

I don't think there's a clean lesson here, except maybe this: the emotion is real data about how you're doing, but it's terrible data about what you should do. Feel it fast. Decide it slow.

And if you're a founder currently drafting the wind-down email in your head — maybe sleep on it one more week.
